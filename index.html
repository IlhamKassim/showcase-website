Project Overview

Three Interactive Displays for Penn State Mechanical Engineering

The Kinetic Engineering Collective designed and built three dynamic, interactive displays installed along the main hallway of the ME Department on the first floor of Reber Building — replacing existing faux vegetation that hadn't been updated since 2016.

The displays are visited daily by students, faculty, staff, alumni, and prospective students and their families. They aim to inspire visitors to appreciate the collaborative spirit and cutting-edge opportunities within Mechanical Engineering at Penn State.

The Three Displays

Each highlighting a different facet of ME at Penn State

Display 1 — Lab Information Panel

Four large panels showcasing key ME topics: Additive/Subtractive Manufacturing, Fluid Flow & Piping, Robotics, and Energy Generation/Storage — with an interactive hydraulic robotic arm and a live solar power output monitor.

Display 2 — Cars & Racing

Features a 2025 Formula SAE car from Penn State's Nittany Motorsports team mounted diagonally in the display space, alongside the Shell Eco-Marathon Team, celebrating real-world student-driven engineering.

Display 3 — Mechanical Counter

An interactive hand-crank visitor counter where each digit represents a progressively more modern technology — from analog gears to wireless signals — visible through a clear Plexiglass panel. Inspired by San Francisco's Exploratorium.

Design Process

Design Process

From brainstorming to the final product

01
Phase 01

Initial Planning

The team established roles, built a Gantt chart for milestone tracking, and set up weekly commitments (10–12 hours/member/week). Project Manager Joshua Bruce created meeting agendas and aligned the team with sponsor Dr. Mary Frecker and faculty coach Dr. Tikyna Dandridge. Undergraduate Lab Manager Drew Mosser was established as a weekly point of contact for materials and logistics.

02
Phase 02

Concept Development

The team generated five concept areas — Power Generation, Robotics, Manufacturing, Automotive Display, and Fluid Flow — and evaluated them using Pugh Concept Scoring against weighted customer needs. Final selections: Display 1 (Lab Info Panel with 4 ME topics), Display 2 (automotive display and racing), and Display 3 (mechanical visitor counter). Visits to The Franklin Institute and Mercedes-AMG motorsport displays provided additional inspiration.

03
Phase 03 · Alpha

Alpha Prototyping

Fully scaled CAD models were created for all three displays. 3D-printed concept panels were produced for Display 1 to test layout and proportions. Initial prototypes were developed for the Cars/Racing display, the Mechanical Counter mechanism, and each of the four sub-topics on the Lab Info Panel. A Bill of Materials and proposed mounting system were submitted to the sponsor.

04
Phase 04 · Beta

Beta Prototyping & Materials

All panels were drafted on final materials. Vinyl graphics were printed through the Learning Factory at no cost. Existing wood paneling was retained for Display 1 to save budget. Electronics were sourced from DigiKey, hardware from McMaster-Carr, and wall panels from Forms+Surfaces — the same company that installed the original wooden wall system in 2024. Structural analysis and safety review confirmed ASME compliance for all mounting systems.

05
Phase 05

Construction & Installation

Full construction of all three displays, coordinated with Reber Building management. The 2025 Nittany Motorsports Formula SAE car was mounted diagonally with steel cables and custom hardware. The mechanical counter was assembled with Schmitt trigger circuitry (based on a 1942 patent) and housed behind 3/8" Plexiglass for safety. Display 1's live solar monitor was connected to Penn State's Nittany 1 solar farm. Target completion: April 22, 2026.

06
Phase 06

Virtual Tour & 360° Documentation

After installation, the hallway was captured using equirectangular 360° photography. The panoramas were processed and loaded into the Pannellum-based virtual tour, allowing remote visitors to walk through the completed displays interactively. Hotspots guide viewers to details about each display and its engineering content.

07
Phase 07

Capstone Showcase & Final Delivery

The completed displays and virtual tour were presented at Penn State's Capstone Showcase — first in-class on April 28, then at the Bryce Jordan Center on April 30, 2026. Final deliverables include a comprehensive Final Report and a Guide Manual covering maintenance, operation, and future updates for all three displays.

Did You Know?

Did You Know?

Fun facts about the project & its impact

Why This Project Matters

The Reber Building's first-floor hallway is visited daily by hundreds of students, faculty, staff, alumni, and prospective students. These displays replace outdated faux vegetation (unchanged since 2016) with something that actually represents the excitement and innovation of Mechanical Engineering at Penn State.

FACT 01

A Real 2025 Formula SAE Car

Display 2 features an actual 2025 race car donated by Penn State's Nittany Motorsports FSAE team — mounted diagonally with steel cables inside the hallway display case. It's the centerpiece of the Cars & Racing exhibit celebrating student-driven engineering.

FACT 02

Live Solar Power in Real Time

Display 1 features a live monitor showing real-time solar power output from Penn State's Nittany 1 solar farm — giving students and visitors a live window into renewable energy generation happening right on campus.

FACT 03

Inspired by the Exploratorium

The mechanical counter on Display 3 was inspired by the Exploratorium's "Your Turn Counts" exhibit in San Francisco. Each digit represents a different era of technology — progressing from analog gears to wireless signals — visible through a clear Plexiglass window.

FACT 04

An 80-Year-Old Circuit Design

The counter's electronics use a Schmitt trigger circuit — a design based on a 1942 patent — to convert mechanical crank motion into digital signals. An 83-year-old invention powering a brand-new interactive display in 2026.

FACT 05

Zero Cost Vinyl Graphics

All graphic panels for Display 1 were printed at no cost through Penn State's Learning Factory. The team also retained the existing wood paneling rather than replacing it — smart design choices that freed up budget for electronics and interactive hardware.

FACT 06

Replacing Displays Untouched Since 2016

The faux vegetation the displays replaced had been in place since 2016 and was shedding extensively — a decade overdue for an upgrade. KEC's displays will serve as a permanent installation, designed for years of continuous, hands-on use by the public.
